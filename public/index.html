Malawi–Zambia Transfrontier Conservation Area

Along the Malawi–Zambia border, five wild species cross paths with the villages that share their land. This is an open-data map of where and when those paths were recorded — assembled as a clean input for the people who study coexistence, not a study itself.

5 conflict-prone species — elephant, lion, hippo, crocodile, buffalo — mapped from the open record
village each record labelled with its nearest named settlement, not just a region
by year scrub the timeline to see when these species were recorded near people

Every point here is one open biodiversity record — where a species was seen or collected, drawn from GBIF — placed against where people live (WorldPop), the parks and reserves around them (OpenStreetMap), and the border that splits this transfrontier landscape between two countries. Each record carries its nearest village and the year it was noted, so the map can show not just where these species turn up but when they turn up near settlement — the timing that coexistence actually turns on. It is not a conflict study, a census, or a hotspot model: those are specialist work with real stakes. This is the data plumbing beneath them, built openly and honestly about its limits, and it runs on hosting anyone in the region can afford. Start with what this is & isn't, or open the map.
